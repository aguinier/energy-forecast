## 2. The finding that changes the shape of ABL-316: wrong source table

`src/db.py:load_renewable_type_data` trains from **`energy_renewable`**.
Every count below says that is the wrong table to extend a 24-country
programme onto.

**History.** Across the 49 trainable pairs:

| | median usable history | pairs with ≥ 365 d |
|---|---:|---:|
| `energy_generation` | **2,049 d** | **49 / 49** |
| `energy_renewable` | 277 d | **10 / 49** |

`energy_renewable` starts 2025-10 or 2025-11 for 20 of the 24 countries. Only
BG, CH, FR (2023-01) and BE (2024-01) reach back further. Retraining 39 new
country/stream pairs from it would train 39 of them on **under nine months** —
a solar or wind model that has never seen a full seasonal cycle.

**NULL vs 0 (required check 1).** This is where the two tables diverge
hardest. `energy_generation.wind_offshore_mw` is **NULL in every row** for 15
countries — the honest encoding of "the TSO does not report this". The same
15 countries in `energy_renewable` read **100.0 % exactly 0.0**, because
`energy_renewable`'s columns are `REAL DEFAULT 0` and its mapper initialises
each one to 0.0 before checking the source frame (ABL-188). A trainer pointed
at `energy_renewable` receives, for AT/BG/CH/CZ/EE/FI/GR/HR/HU/LT/LV/RO/SE/SI/SK,
a complete, non-null, perfectly "valid" all-zero offshore series and no signal
that anything is wrong. Nothing in the read path distinguishes it from a real
measurement.

**Zero-fill, corroborated (required check 2).** For each pair I applied
ABL-188's own adjudication test: a 0.0 in `energy_renewable` is contamination
only where the NaN-preserving sibling has *materially* non-zero generation at
the identical timestamp (threshold 1 % of that series' own 99th percentile —
a bare `> 0` test marks ordinary night-time metering noise as contamination
and inflated DE solar from 2.8 k to 15.3 k rows before I put the threshold in).

Result: **6,711 provably zero-filled rows across 35 of 72 pairs.** Largest:
DE solar 2,773 rows peaking at a contradicted 43,408.5 MW, AT wind_onshore
588, ES wind_onshore 554, NL wind_onshore 521, NL wind_offshore 447.

The 24-hour minimum in `exclude_suspect_constant_runs` does not catch all of
it. LV solar has 374 zero-filled rows and **zero** runs ≥ 24 h; EE
wind_onshore 33 and zero; NO/FI/GR/SE wind_onshore likewise. Those rows pass
the guard and enter training as measured zeros. This is a real limit of the
ABL-188 guard, and it argues the same way as everything else in this section.

**Duplicate instants — new, not previously filed.** `energy_renewable`'s
UNIQUE index is on `(country_code, timestamp_utc)` as a *string*, so
`'2025-11-09 23:00:00'`, `'2025-11-09T23:00:00'` and
`'2025-11-09T00:00:00+01:00'` are three distinct keys for one instant and all
three can be stored. Measured: **78,510 duplicate-instant rows in
`energy_renewable` across all 24 countries, of which 5,425 instants carry
disagreeing values** — the table holds two different answers for the same
hour and nothing downstream chooses between them. Worst: NL 601 disagreeing
instants, AT 238, HU 209, BG 122, SE 111. `energy_generation` has **0**
duplicate instants. AT wind_onshore at `2025-11-17 16:45` is the illustrative
case: `energy_renewable` stores both `0.0` and `3,788.0`.

**Recommendation (for the CEO, not an action I take here):** point the
individual-renewable training path at `energy_generation` before ABL-316's
retrain issues run. That is a change to `src/db.py:load_renewable_type_data`
in the forecast module — not ingest, not dashboard — but it is a source
switch under a Board directive, so it is your call, and it should be its own
issue with its own before/after check on the four countries that already have
models. If the answer is no, the retrain issues should be scoped to the ~10
pairs that have ≥ 365 d in `energy_renewable`, not to 24 countries.

## 3. Contamination and gaps in the source I do recommend

`energy_generation` is not perfect, but it is close, and the damage is
localised:

- **Longest gap anywhere: FR, 2026-06-30 23:45 → 2026-07-22 14:15, 518.5 h
  (21.6 days).** FR is the *only* country with a gap over 7 days in any of
  the three streams. It ended three weeks before this audit and it is
  recent enough to matter for anything trained or scored now. **Not covered
  by ABL-71/67/111/109** — flagging it as new.
- **ES, 2025-04-28 10:15 → 2025-04-29 22:00, 35.8 h.** The Iberian
  Peninsula blackout. Real event, correctly absent, not contamination.
- Everything else: longest gap ≤ 9.3 h.

**The "FR 5-month 2023 gap" reference case does not reproduce as stated.** FR
`solar` and `wind_onshore` are complete through 2023 in both tables (8,757
hourly rows in 2023), and FR `energy_load` is complete too (8,760 rows). The
five-month 2023 absence that *does* exist is in FR **`wind_offshore`**: the
column is NULL for 21,550 rows from 2021-01-01 until first report on
2023-05-31 22:00, i.e. Jan–May 2023 is the tail of a NULL prefix. That is
fleet commissioning, not a data fault. If the reference case meant a
different table, my detector will find it — point me at the table and I will
re-run.

**Known contamination touching these windows:** ABL-188 (DE solar
`energy_renewable`) — reproduced exactly, see §4; it touches the
`energy_renewable` census only, not the `energy_generation` verdicts.
ABL-71 (stale prod ingest), ABL-67 (`net_position`) and ABL-111/109
(zero-as-missing actual **load**) do not touch `energy_generation`'s solar /
wind_onshore / wind_offshore columns and are not implicated in any verdict
here.

## 4. Precedent check — the detector re-finds the known runs

Required check 2 says the detector is wrong if it cannot re-find ABL-188 and
ABL-198/199/200. Both reproduce, and the second one corrects the record.

**ABL-188 (DE solar, `energy_renewable`): exact reproduction.** One zero run
≥ 24 h, `2025-09-08 22:00 → 2025-11-14 15:45`, **6,408 rows**, 1,601.75 h.
That matches the ABL-188 adjudication report, not the 5,096 quoted in this
issue's brief — 5,096 was an earlier count taken before 2025-11-01..11-14 had
synced, and `reports/abl_188_solar_zero_adjudication.md` already supersedes
it. Corroboration: 2,773 of those rows have materially non-zero
`energy_generation` at the same timestamp, peaking at **43,408.5 MW** on
2025-09-19 11:15 — the same 43.4 GW the ABL-188 report cites.

**ABL-198/199/200 (BE wind_offshore): re-found, but 8 of the 9 flagged runs
are not contamination.** The detector finds 9 zero runs ≥ 24 h totalling 320
rows. Corroborating each against `energy_generation`:

| run | rows | `energy_generation` over the same window |
|---|---:|---|
| 2024-03-05 → 03-06 | 30 | max **−7.0** MW |
| 2024-05-08 → 05-10 | 39 | max **−6.2** MW |
| 2024-08-16 → 08-18 | 41 | max **−14.3** MW |
| 2024-12-27 → 12-28 | 26 | max **−12.5** MW |
| 2025-01-15 → 01-16 | 26 | max **−13.6** MW |
| 2025-03-02 → 03-04 | 43 | max **−15.6** MW |
| 2025-04-17 → 04-18 | 29 | max **−9.7** MW |
| **2025-11-15 20:00 → 11-17 17:00** | **46** | **max 2,175.3 MW — real generation** |
| 2026-03-08 → 03-10 | 40 | max **−11.4** MW |

Eight of the nine sit on windows where the sibling table is *also* at or

unambiguous. A run detector without the sibling-table corroboration
over-reports BE offshore contamination by 7×; that corroboration is now a
column in the audit output (`ren_zerofill_rows`).

## 5. Artifact vs served rows (required check 5) — the DE offshore question

**DE is on the "trained but serving nothing" side of the gap, and the cause
is a missing file, not a data problem.**

`Forecaster.load` (`src/forecaster.py:883`) reads exactly
`models/<CC>/<type>/model.joblib`. For DE wind_offshore that file **does not
exist**. What does exist:

```
models/DE/wind_offshore/candidate/model.joblib     662,350 B  2026-02-24
models/DE/wind_offshore/centroid/model.joblib
models/DE/wind_offshore/multipoint/model.joblib
models/DE/wind_offshore/production/model.joblib    662,350 B  2026-02-24
models/DE/wind_offshore/model.joblib               MISSING
```

BE and FR both have the top-level `model.joblib` (5.6 MB, 2025-12-26) *and*
the variants. So `forecast_daily.py` catches `FileNotFoundError`, logs
`[SKIP] ... Model not trained yet`, and DE offshore writes **0 rows** to
`forecasts` — confirmed against the replica: `wind_offshore` has 31,876 rows
for BE and 30,864 for FR, and none for DE, while DE `solar` and
`wind_onshore` both wrote 30,864 rows at 14:00 today.

So a trained, promoted-to-`production/` DE offshore model exists and the
serving path never looks at it. Nothing is wrong with DE's offshore data:
196,756 non-null observations, 0.07 % zeros, peak 8,486.4 MW, no gap, no
suspect run — verdict `TRAIN`.

**ABL-319 reached the same conclusion independently, and closed on it** — its
answer landed at 18:17 UTC on 2026-08-12, eleven minutes before this audit's
final run, from the serving side rather than the artifact side. It adds the
half I could not see: there is **no country gate anywhere between the
`forecasts` table and the chart**, so the ABL-316 retrains are a pure training
exercise with no plumbing component. Two independent routes to the same
finding is worth more than either alone; I have not duplicated a comment onto
a closed issue.

Note the audit script had the same bug I am reporting: a directory-existence
check called DE offshore "already covered". It now requires the file
`Forecaster.load` actually reads, and reports orphaned variants explicitly.

## 7. Two caveats I would not want acted on unknowingly

**NL solar is not national solar.** NL's peak reported `solar_mw` over the
whole window is **428.8 MW**, against a peak load of 19,632 MW — a ratio of
**0.022**. Every comparable country sits between 0.10 and 0.71 (DE 0.71, ES
0.71, BE 0.60, PL 0.52, IT 0.50, FR 0.25, HR 0.13, SE 0.10). The Dutch
installed fleet is well over 20 GW. The ENTSO-E series therefore covers only
a small grid-metered subset. NL solar is internally consistent and perfectly
forecastable, so the verdict stays `TRAIN` — but a model trained on it
forecasts that metered subset, and it must not be labelled or charted as
Dutch solar generation. Whoever ships NL solar to the dashboard needs to know
this.

**Three sub-50 MW streams are excluded on a threshold, not on absence.** NO
solar (peak 7.5 MW), SI wind_onshore (4.8 MW) and SK wind_onshore (3.1 MW)
are real, reported, mostly clean series. I called them `EXCLUDE-NO-FLEET`
against an explicit **50 MW** threshold on all-time peak, on the grounds that
a demonstrator-scale stream cannot repay a model and sits below the noise
floor of everything else on the dashboard. The threshold is a judgement, it
is stated in the script as `NEGLIGIBLE_FLEET_MW`, and the peaks are in the
table — override it and those three become `TRAIN`.

**One verdict beyond the six in the brief.** PL wind_offshore is
`EXCLUDE-INSUFFICIENT-HISTORY`. Calling clean 38-day data `CONTAMINATED`
would be false and `NO-DATA` would be false. It is one row; rename it if you
prefer.
