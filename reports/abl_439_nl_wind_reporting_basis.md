## Verdict

NL `wind_onshore` did not lose two thirds of its output. **The series changes
reporting basis at `2026-07-01 17:00:00 UTC`, as a step one sampling interval
wide**, and the level either side is a function of **how old each value was when
we fetched it**, not of the wind.

The fit window sits **94.78%** on the *revised* basis and the gate window sits
**100%** on the *unrevised* basis. They are not measuring the same quantity, so
every ABL-348 comparison that spans them is comparing two different series.

Two consequences, stated up front because they outrank the grade question:

1. **G2 and G3 are vacuous for NL** (Section 6). Both compare the challenger to a
   level taken from the fit window -- 728.03 MW -- against a gate window whose mean
   is 224.58 MW. Beating that reference is arithmetic. G1 and G4 are computed
   entirely inside the gate window and **survive**.
2. **NL's gate-window actuals are provisional.** Not one of the 2,880 gate rows
   has been fetched past the revision horizon (max age 18.74 days against a
   horizon of ~28). The ingest's own measured behaviour predicts they will rise by
   roughly 2.2x at the next backfill, moving every NL number in tranche 2e --
   including the two conditions that currently survive.

And one the issue did not ask for but which the same screen returns:
**two further committed graded pairs carry this signature** -- NL `solar`
(tranche 2d, grade B) and GR `solar` (tranche 2c, grade C). NL `solar`'s
`constant_causal` bias is **-1.62%**, so no screen based on the size of the
constant would ever have found it (Section 5).

Read the `max` column, not just the mean. A calm month lowers a mean; it does not

from 2024-01 through 2026-06 and has not exceeded 996.55 MW since. That is a
ceiling moving, which is a property of what is being counted.

`energy_renewable` shows **no step at all** -- it has carried the low basis for its
entire NL history (from 2025-11-09). The two tables converge because
`energy_generation` drops onto `energy_renewable`, not because both moved.

**Attribution under the ABL-396 pattern:** the shift is present in **one** table,
so it is not upstream of the source mapping in the sense that pattern means.
Section 4 shows it is not *in* the mapping either -- the mapping is provably
constant across the step. It is a third thing: the two tables hold different
**vintages** of the same published series.

## 2. Step or ramp, and its date

**A step.** Transition width **0.25 h** -- one 15-minute sampling interval, which
is as sharp as this source can resolve.

| | `energy_generation` | `energy_renewable` | ratio |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| last disagreeing instant `2026-07-01 16:45` | 286.06 | 51.65 | 5.54 |
| first agreeing instant `2026-07-01 17:00` | 49.57 | 49.60 | 1.00 |

Dated by a changepoint over daily relative differences (98.54% purity over 274
days: 235 before, 39 after), then refined to the source's own cadence. Aggregate
ratio 2.3032 before, 0.9943 after; the largest absolute disagreement anywhere
after the step is **13.40 MW**.

> The dating method matters here. A per-instant *relative* agreement test dates
> this step five weeks late, at `2026-08-09`, because 164 post-step hours sit at
> single-digit MW where a 13 MW offset reads as a 40-140% difference. Daily means
> separate the two states by ~20x and have no such failure mode. The script
> documents this at `AGREEMENT_TOLERANCE`.

## 3. Which level is implausible -- the independent cross-check

`energy_generation_forecast` holds the TSO's own day-ahead forecast for the same
fleet. It is published by the TSO, is not derived from the actuals, and is not
touched by whatever moved them. Dividing the actuals by it removes the weather: a
calm month lowers both terms and leaves the ratio flat.

| month | TSO fc | actual/TSO on `energy_generation` | actual/TSO on `energy_renewable` |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| 2026-04 | 1027.58 | 0.655 | 0.251 |
| 2026-05 | 756.81 | 0.713 | 0.270 |
| 2026-06 | 975.24 | 0.687 | 0.294 |
| **2026-07** | 868.26 | **0.303** | 0.298 |
| **2026-08** | 618.16 | **0.338** | 0.343 |

**The TSO forecast does not step.** It declines smoothly with the season
(975 -> 868 -> 618 MW), exactly as a real July/August calm looks. The actuals
ratio, which should be flat, drops by a factor of 2.2 in one month.

Control -- DE, same measure, same months: `generation/TSO` = 1.008, 1.018, 0.970,
1.017. Flat and at unity, so the reference is sound and the NL reading is not an
artefact of the method.

Long-run, `energy_generation` against the TSO forecast for NL:

| 2021 | 2022 | 2023 | 2024 | 2025 | 2026 |
|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 0.63 | 0.51 | 0.50 | 0.61 | 0.66 | 0.61 |

**So the gate-window level is the anomalous one.** The fit-window level is the one
consistent with six years of NL history.

**But do not read the fit-window level as "correct".** At 0.50-0.66 of the TSO's
own forecast, NL's published actuals have *never* matched the TSO's view of the
same fleet -- consistent with NL onshore wind being largely distributed and only
partly metered, which is the ABL-325 shape one series over. Both NL bases are
subsets; the revised one is the less incomplete subset. That is why "just refit on
the low basis" is not automatically the right repair either.

## 4. Who owns it: the basis is a function of age at fetch

`fetched_at` dates the retrieval of each stored value. For NL `energy_generation`,
**one backfill session on 2026-07-29 (194,788 rows, covering 2021-01-01 to
2026-07-23) wrote values on both sides of the step.** One session, one code
version, one mapper -- and two bases out of it. Our mapping is therefore *provably
not the author*: the same code produced both.

What the same session does separate is **age at fetch**, and it separates it
perfectly:

| rows | n | age at fetch (days) |
|---|---:|---|
| fit window, pre-step | 16,196 | **28.05 - 196.74** |
| fit window, post-step | 892 | **18.75 - 28.03** |
| gate window (all) | 2,880 | 3.59 - 18.74 |

**Not one row violates the boundary**, which sits between 28.03 and 28.05 days.
The step's date, `2026-07-01 17:00`, is simply `2026-07-29 17:49` (the backfill)
minus **27.3 days**.

This is a **revision horizon of about four weeks**. `energy_renewable` is written
by a daily D+1 job and has therefore *only ever* held the unrevised vintage, which
is why it shows no step and sits at 0.25-0.34 of the TSO forecast throughout.

Separate what is measured from what is inferred here. **Measured, on the replica:**
the stored level is a function of age at fetch, with a boundary between 28.03 and
28.05 days and no exceptions in 19,968 rows, inside a single fetch session. **Not
measured:** *why* the upstream returns a larger number after four weeks. The
natural reading is settlement metering replacing real-time metering for a fleet
that is largely distributed -- which fits NL onshore wind, fits the direction, and
fits ABL-325's finding one series over -- but confirming it means re-querying
ENTSO-E at two ages for the same instant, which is an ingest action and not this
role's to take. The attribution above does not depend on it: whatever the upstream
reason, the two windows hold different vintages, and that is enough to void a
comparison that spans them.

Two things follow.

- **Nothing here is a defect in the gate harness**, as the issue anticipated. The
  harness computed 225.54% correctly from the rows it was given.
- **The remaining question is for whoever owns ingest**, not for this module:
  should the daily job re-fetch a trailing window past the revision horizon, so
  stored actuals converge to the settled vintage instead of freezing whichever
  vintage happened to be current at D+1? Today the answer is decided implicitly by
  when someone last ran a backfill. Filing that is proposed under
  "Recommendations" rather than done here, since ingest is out of scope for this
  role.

## 6. Consequence for tranche 2e's grade A

NL `wind_onshore` is graded **A** in all three bands on
`experiments/ABL348/results_abl417_tranche2e.json`. Taking the 24-36h cell
(n = 720, gate basis `challenger, seasonal_naive`, out-of-sample):

| | test | value | status |
|---|---|---|---|
| **G1** | beats `seasonal_naive` D-7 | skill 17.84%, own-error margin 21.71% vs a 7.51% floor | **survives** |
| **G2** | beats `constant_causal` | reference is 728.03 MW against a 224.58 MW target | **vacuous** |
| **G3** | beats `climatology_causal` | same fit-window level, per hour of day | **vacuous** |
| **G4** | `slope > 0` and `corr > 0` | slope 0.4435, corr 0.5907 | **survives** |

G1 and G4 are computed entirely inside the gate window. The D-7 lookback from
`2026-07-11` reaches `2026-07-04`, which is post-step, so seasonal-naive is
basis-consistent with its target. Both conditions are real measurements.

G2 and G3 take their level from the fit window and are therefore comparisons
against a series the gate window does not contain. They are not *passed*; they are
**not measured**. Under the ABL-418 rule that a condition which could not be
measured is not satisfied and is named like any other failure, that is the shape of
a **B**, not an **A** -- but re-grading is ABL-437's call and this pack does not
make it.

Worth recording in the model's favour: the challenger was itself fitted on 94.78%
superseded-basis rows and carries the level forward -- gate bias **+45.91%**
(24-36h) and **+46.40%** (36-48h), about +103 MW on a 225 MW series, exactly the
carry-over this diagnosis predicts. It cleared D-7 readably anyway. G1 is a
*harder* pass than it looks, not a softer one.

**And G1/G4 are themselves provisional**, per the Verdict: the gate actuals they
are computed against are the unrevised vintage.

## 7. Contamination screen

- **ABL-188** (`energy_renewable` zero-fills a type ENTSO-E did not return):
  **clear**. Monthly zero fraction over both windows and both tables is 0.0000
  except `energy_renewable` 2026-03 (0.0027) and 2026-06 (0.0014). No
  bit-identical constant run. A zero-fill cannot produce this shift in any case --
  it would drive a series to zero, and the low basis is a live, weather-correlated
  series.
- **ABL-71** (prod ingest stale, fixes undeployed): related in kind, distinct in
  mechanism. This is a revision-horizon effect in the replica's own fetch history,
  not the staleness ABL-71 describes.
- **ABL-67** (fabricated `net_position` rows), **ABL-109 / ABL-111**
  (zero-as-missing actual-load rows): different tables and different types. Do not
  touch this series.

## 8. Recommendations

Recommendations only. Promotion, source registration and ingest changes are not
this role's to make.

1. **Put a named data hold on NL `wind_onshore` in the ABL-316 ledger.** Its three
   grade-A cells rest on two measured conditions and two vacuous ones, against
   provisional actuals.
2. **Hand the ladder question to ABL-437** with G2/G3 marked *not measurable on
   this pair* rather than *passed*. This pack deliberately does not re-grade.
3. **Do not re-read NL on `energy_generation` at the registered fit window.** It
   would reproduce the same defect. If NL is re-read, `energy_renewable` is the
   basis-consistent option (continuous from 2025-11-09, causal constant 92.24%) --
   which inverts ABL-348's source choice for this one pair and is therefore a new
   pre-registration, not a flag. Restricting the fit window to post-step
   `energy_generation` is not viable: 9.3 days.
4. **File the revision-horizon question against ingest** (Founding Engineer, via
   the CEO): should the daily job re-fetch a trailing window past the ~28-day
   horizon? Until it does, the boundary between vintages in `energy_generation` is
   set by when someone last ran a backfill, and any window straddling it compares
   two different series. This is not NL-only in principle.
5. **Extend the hold to NL `solar` (tranche 2d, grade B) and GR `solar`
   (tranche 2c, grade C).** Both are committed graded pairs carrying the same
   signature, at 1.63x and 0.79x respectively. NL `solar` is the important one:
   its near-zero `constant_causal` bias means no bias-based screen will ever
   surface it, and it is the series ABL-325 already found to be a metered subset.
   Their grades are not A, so nothing promotion-eligible moves -- but a C earned
   against a fit window on a superseded basis is no more readable than an A.
6. **Screen the remaining flagged pairs before they are read**, none of which is a
   registered ABL-316 type today: LV biomass (1.50), CY solar (0.64), CY
   wind_onshore (0.81), NL biomass (1.15).
7. **Screen on the source-table ratio, not on the constant.** The one-line check
   is `--sweep`; it is cheap, it needs no fit, and it is the only column here that
   detects NL `solar`.
